WHAT TO TRY & WHERE TO FIND IT

A LITTLE BIT OF EVERYWHERE

What to eat while you’re here

Mauritian food mixes Indian, Creole, Chinese and French cooking, and the best of it is sold from street stalls for a few rupees. Here’s what we’d love you to try.

THE GREAT FOOD DEBATE

Bride vs groom

SONALI’S GO-TO

Mine frite

Fried noodles with egg and chilli: the plate Sonali orders every single time.

&

PIERS’S GO-TO

Biryani

A big plate of Mauritian biryani, potatoes and all. Piers won’t say no.

Dholl puri

The island’s favourite snack: a soft flatbread made with ground split peas, wrapped around bean curry, chutney and pickles.

Street stalls & markets

Roti chaud

A thin, warm roti rolled around butter bean curry, rougaille or vindaye.

Street stalls

♥ Sonali’s favourite

Boulettes

Steamed dumplings of fish, chayote or taro, served in broth with chilli paste.

China Town, Port Louis

♥ Sonali’s favourite

Mine frite

Mauritian-Chinese fried noodles with egg, chilli and soy.

Street stalls & restaurants

Bol renversé

The “upside-down bowl”: rice topped with a stir-fry and a fried egg, turned out onto the plate.

Mauritian-Chinese restaurants

Farata

The Mauritian paratha, flaky and buttery, eaten with curry or pickled vegetables.

Markets & roadside stalls

♥ Piers’s favourite

Biryani

Layers of spiced rice with chicken, lamb or fish, and potatoes.

Restaurants & markets

Cari & rougaille

Mauritian curry, and rougaille, a Creole tomato sauce with garlic, ginger, thyme and chilli.

Most restaurants

Fish vindaye

Fried fish pickled with mustard seed, turmeric and garlic. Tangy and very moreish.

Creole restaurants

Octopus vindaye

Vindaye ourite: octopus in the same tangy mustard-seed and turmeric sauce as the fish, often eaten cold with bread.

Creole & seaside restaurants

Pineapple with chilli salt

Sweet Victoria pineapple on a stick, dusted with chilli and salt. Sounds odd, tastes amazing.

Beach vendors

Alouda

A chilled milky drink with basil seeds, agar jelly and syrup. Perfect on a hot afternoon.

Port Louis Central Market

GRAB AND GO

Street snacks

Gâteaux piment

Little crispy chilli fritters made from split peas. Dip them in coriander chutney.

★ Sonali recommended
Pain kebab

Spit-roasted chicken in a baguette with salad, onion and chilli. Shabaan in Quatre Bornes is the famous one.

Bajia

Crispy chickpea-flour fritters with spring onion, Mauritius’s answer to pakoras.

Samoussa

Little fried pastries filled with spiced potato, fish, chicken or cheese.

Gato arouille

Crispy taro fritters with a hint of ginger, a little sweet and a little salty.

Pain Frit

Classic crunchy fried bread snack to be served hot, paired with spicy chatini (a fresh coriander and tomato chutney).

SOMETHING SWEET

For the sweet tooth

★ Sonali recommended
Banana tart

Tarte banane: pastry filled with sweet stewed banana under a criss-cross top. A bakery classic.

Napolitaine

Two shortbread biscuits sandwiched with jam and covered in pink icing.

Gato patate

Little sweet-potato pastries, often filled with coconut.

Gato coco

Chewy coconut sweets, usually pink and white.

TO DRINK

Something to wash it down

Phoenix beer

The local lager, brewed since 1963.

Mauritian rum

Try a Ti’ Punch, or rhum arrangé steeped with fruit and spices.

Vanilla tea

Mauritian tea, often flavoured with vanilla. Taste it at Bois Chéri.

Fresh coconut

Sold green and chilled from roadside stands.

★ Sonali recommended
Eski

A Mauritian fizzy drink in four flavours that locals grew up on.

★ Sonali recommended
Fanta orange

Somehow even better ice-cold on a Mauritian beach.

OUR SHORTLIST

Where to eat

STREET FOOD

Dewa & Sons, Rose Hill

A famous dholl puri stop. Takeaway, cheap and always busy.

Port Louis Central Market

Dholl puri, alouda and boulettes in one place. Go in the morning.

Shabaan, Quatre Bornes

Often called the best pain kebab on the island. Expect a lunchtime queue; it’s worth it.

SIT-DOWN LUNCHES

First Restaurant, China Town

Busy Cantonese spot with lots of dim sum. Book on Sundays.

Escale Créole, Moka

Home-style Creole cooking in a garden, with homemade infused rums.

Maison Eureka, Moka

Lunch on the veranda of a 180-year-old plantation house.

Dragon Vert, Quatre Bornes

A family favourite for Chinese-Mauritian food. Try the Magic Bowl or the riz dragon.

WITH A VIEW

La Table du Château

At Château de Labourdonnais. Try the vanilla crème brûlée.

L’Alchimiste, Chamarel

At the Rhumerie de Chamarel, with rum-laced desserts.

Le Chamarel Restaurant

Lunch looking out to Le Morne and the sea.

Domaine Anna, Flic en Flac

Reimagined Chinese dishes and seafood, served in gazebos over the lakes. Also where our Mehendi is!
